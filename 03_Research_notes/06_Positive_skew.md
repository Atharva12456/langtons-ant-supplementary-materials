### Smaller all-width wake restrictions for positive-skew band C

[upper-F122 quiet-six lemma](06_Positive_skew.md#source-research-notes-positive-skew-upper-thin-quiet6-requires-quiet7-2026-10-01-md)

Upper F122 therefore has quiet seven; if upper four is quiet, upper
eight must wake whether six is quiet or wake. Five exact endpoint
families remain under quiet four, classified in that note and the
[upper-six note](06_Positive_skew.md#source-research-notes-positive-skew-bandc-upper6-wake8-reduction-2026-10-01-md).
These later restrictions do not change section 3's four schedules for
the different upper F123 cap. The whole orbit remains open.

Work with literal drift `(1,3)`, growth eight, and `t=3x-y`, with the
minimum transverse level normalized to zero. The lower `(1,2,3)` band C
forces wakes `0,2,3,4`. Write `W` for the even width. The established
width exclusion gives `W>=18`. The whole drift orbit remains **open**.

This note gives arithmetic lemmas valid at every width and period. It
also saves a smaller necessary upper-word domain for subsequent work.
It neither constructs a highway nor proves that its surviving words can
be joined across the strip.

#### 1. Only one disjoint opposite-checker pair is possible

Label a wake by its fibre `j=-t mod3` and sign `epsilon=(-1)^t`.
P22 requires the signed wake sum in each of the three fibres to equal
two modulo four. Each fibre therefore contains at least two wakes.
With eight total wakes, their unsigned sizes must be `4,2,2`.
A two-wake fibre has equal signs; a four-wake fibre has a `3+1` sign
split. These statements follow by listing the signed sums at sizes two
and four, not from a search over widths.

Consequently there cannot be two **disjoint** opposite-sign pairs,
whether they lie in the same fibre or different fibres. The two smaller
fibres contain no such pair, and the larger fibre has only one minority
sign wake, which every opposite-sign pair must share. This distinction
matters: several pairs sharing that one wake are allowed.

In band C, `0` and `3` are already an opposite-sign pair in fibre zero.
The wakes `2` and `4` supply a positive wake to each other fibre.
Thus the complete fibre sign counts have exactly the following two forms:

| Fibre | Positive-dominant zero fibre | Negative-dominant zero fibre |
|---|---|---|
| zero | 3 positive, 1 negative | 1 positive, 3 negative |
| one | 2 positive, 0 negative | 2 positive, 0 negative |
| two | 2 positive, 0 negative | 2 positive, 0 negative |

Every odd wake is therefore `3 mod6`. In the first case level three is
the only odd wake; in the second case level zero is the only positive
wake in fibre zero. This is a global restriction on all eight wakes.

#### 2. Upper offset three is quiet for both remaining upper caps

The upper extremal wake `W` is positive. A hypothetical wake at `W-3`
would be negative and lie in the same fibre as `W`. Since `W>=18`,
that pair is disjoint from the lower pair `0,3`. Section 1 excludes it.

Hence **band C forces quiet upper inward offset three**, independently
of whether the upper cap is `(1,2,2)` or `(1,2,3)`. This extends the
previous quiet-three result to the upper `(1,2,2)` case. It does not
exclude either upper cap when that offset is quiet.

#### 3. Four upper schedules for an upper `(1,2,3)` cap

Use the exact seven endpoint families in
[the earlier classification](06_Positive_skew.md#source-research-notes-positive-skew-fatc-fat123-wake-families-2026-09-30-md).
Its fixed upper wakes are `W-2,W`, with upper offset one quiet. Its
extra wakes through inward offset nine are **exactly**

```text

```

In particular offsets `3,5,6,9` are quiet. Wake eight forces wake
four, `W=2 mod6`, and the sole residual wake `W-8`. Wake seven forces
quiet four, `W=4 mod6`, and two odd residual wakes, the larger at `W-7`.
These implications involve only wake positions, not visit multiplicities.

Here is an all-width derivation directly from that table. If offset
four is quiet, its first two families have residual distances from W
at least twelve/ten and fourteen/ten, respectively. The third family's
even residual distances are at least ten. In the fourth family,
`W=6m+4` and residuals are `6r+3` with `r<=m-1`; their upper
distances are `6(m-r)+1>=7`. Only distance seven can occur through
nine, at the larger parameter `p=m-1`.

If offset four is wake, the three families have sole residual distances
`6(m-q)`, `6(m-q)+2`, and `6(m-q)+4`, with `1<=q<=m-1`.
In the first family the only possible distance through nine would be
six, at `q=m-1`, but the required even parity of `m+q` excludes it.
In the second family distance eight occurs exactly at `q=m-1`, and
the required odd parity is automatic. The third family's distance is
at least ten. This proves every exclusion just stated without a
bounded-width assumption.

All four arithmetic schedules occur. The following examples pass P22,

#### 4. Band C with upper `(1,2,2)` also has quiet offset five


rank stays odd. This contradicts the required even rank.

remaining upper cap**. For upper `(1,2,2)` this proof combines P22,

#### Endpoint classification under quiet upper four

Lower fat band C fixes wakes `0,2,3,4`. Its fibre-zero sign counts are
either `(3 positive,1 negative)` or `(1 positive,3 negative)`;
fibres one and two each have two positive wakes. Here
`j=-t mod3`, `n=ceil(t/3)`, and the checker sign is `(-1)^t`.
These counts follow from growth-eight P22 and the lower opposite-sign
pair `0,3`. They forbid negative wakes outside fibre zero.

Consider an upper `(1,2,2)` cap with quiet inward offset four and wake
inward offset six. The cap itself has only its extremal wake W through
offset two. Established width exclusion gives `W>=18`.

The two positive wakes `W-6,W` belong to the same fibre. If that were
fibre one or two, they and the existing lower wake `2` or `4` would
give three positive wakes in a fibre that has exactly two. Thus
`W=6m`. Fibre zero is now filled by `0,3,W-6,W`, with positive majority.
The two remaining wakes must be positive, one in each other fibre:

```text
O={0,2,3,4,6p+2,6q+4,W-6,W},   W=6m.
```

The residuals lie above level four. Quiet upper two gives
`1<=q<=m-2`; quiet upper four gives `1<=p<=m-2`. Both residuals
therefore precede `W-6`.

The ordered fixed fibre word is `0,1,0,2,0,0`, with four
increasing-fibre pairs. The fibre-one residual adds two interactions
with fixed wakes, the fibre-two residual adds three, and their mutual
pair contributes one exactly when `p<=q`. The required even rank
therefore forces `p<=q`.

The signed longitudinal moment is

```text
M=4m+2(p+q)+3 = 3+2(p+q) (mod4).
```

Requiring M=1 gives odd `p+q`, hence p and q differ. The exact
arithmetic family is consequently

```text
1<=p<q<=m-2,   p+q odd.
```

Conversely every such pair satisfies P22, moment and rank. This is
sufficiency for those arithmetic conditions only.

The residual upper distances are `6(m-p)-2>=16` and
`6(m-q)-4>=8`. The four lower fixed distances are at least twenty,
since the bounds require `m>=4`. Besides fixed wake six, the only
possible upper wake through offset fifteen is eight, occurring exactly
when `q=m-2`. In particular offsets `3,4,5,7,9,10,11,12,13,14,15`
are quiet.

#### 1. Coordinates, extrema, and exact matching

The physical moves change `t` by

| Move | E | W | N | S |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Change in t | +3 | -3 | -1 | +1 |

At the minimum level a horizontal arrival can only be W. Its R departure is
N, outside the strip, so every such visit would be L. Stable first R rules
this out. A vertical arrival there can only be N, and its only allowed turn
is R. Alternation then makes the class a singleton NR. At the maximum,
the same direct argument gives a singleton SR. Both extrema are vertical,
so their level difference W is even. Normalize the minimum to level zero;
even levels have vertical arrivals and odd levels horizontal arrivals.

Use representatives `c_t=(0,-t)`. For a phase event at `c_t+k*d`, its lifted
time is `tau=i-N*k`. Edge voltages are

```
lambda(E)=1, lambda(W)=-1, lambda(N)=lambda(S)=0,
tau(target)=tau(source)+1-N*lambda.
```

Every fixed directed edge block preserves stable order, since its time shift
is constant. Thus its first departure feeds its first arrival, its second
departure its second arrival, and so on. This ordinal matching retains the
physical copy voltage; it is not arbitrary pairing of quotient ports.

If a forced path returns to its starting physical cell before visiting that
cell anywhere in between, its endpoint is the immediately next stable event
in that class. In particular, its endpoint turn is opposite. The path
`W,N,E,S` has displacement zero and voltage `-1+0+1+0=0`; its endpoint
lifted time is exactly the starting time plus four, for every N.

#### 2. Exhaustive first-three-level profiles

Level one has no east arrival, and it supplies the unique northward entry

Its SL count equals the level-one WL count. Alternation gives exactly:

| Profile | Level 0 | Level 1 | Level 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| missing (1,1,0) | NR | WR | empty |
| guard (1,1,1) | NR | WR | NR |
| fat (1,2,2) | NR | WR,WL | NR,SL |
| fat (1,2,3) | NR | WR,WL | NR,SL,NR |

This is exhaustive without any upper bound on width, period, or lifetimes.

#### 3. Missing-cap exclusion and guard normal form

Let U be the unique west departure on level four when level one is WR.
It feeds that WR. Level zero then feeds the unique east arrival on level
three. If this east arrival is ER, there is the forced physical return

```
U -> 1:WR -> 0:NR -> 3:ER -> 4:V,
       W       N       E       S.
```

There is no intermediate level-four event. Consequently U,V are consecutive
stable events with opposite turns. If U=NL, V=SR is a distinct second west
departure, contradicting uniqueness. If U=SR, V=SL occurs after a south
arrival U. Hence **the level-three ER cannot be its first south departure**:
otherwise ordinal matching makes V the first south arrival, contradicting U.

At a missing cap, level two is empty. Level three has one east arrival and
zero north departures. Its only possible words are ER and ER,WL. Its ER
is the first south departure in both. The preceding square excludes both.
Therefore the missing cap is impossible.

At a guard cap, level three has one east arrival and one north departure.
Its port counts are ER=WR=1, EL=0, and WL=2-a, where `a=omega_3`.
Its ER-first orders are excluded by the same square. Thus

```
3: WR,WL,ER,[WL]^(1-a).
```

The ER is its second south departure. Since U cannot be NL, it is SR,
immediately followed by the second south arrival SL. There is only one west
departure on level four, so NL=0. Its first south arrival is U=SR. No event
can precede this SR: a preceding L would be an earlier SL, while a preceding
NR alone would violate alternation. All remaining ports alternate NR,SL.
Writing `b=omega_4` gives the complete guard form

```
4: SR,SL,(NR,SL)^(1-a),[NR]^b.
```

The upper statements follow by the orientation-preserving physical half-turn
`(x,y)->(-x,-y)`. It swaps E/W and N/S, preserves R/L, and swaps the extrema.
In particular an upper guard begins

```
0: SR;  1: ER;  2: SR;
3: ER,EL,WR,[EL]^(1-a);
4: NR,NL,(SR,NL)^(1-a),[SR]^b.
```

This use of a half-turn is not a reflection and does not exchange R with L.
The missing-cap proof uses zero voltage only, so reversing the drift by this
half-turn adds no chronology assumption.

#### 4. Lower-guard exclusion at every period

This argument needs positive drift (1,3), not its reversed drift. Let
`a=omega_3`, `b=omega_4`, `c=omega_5`, and retain the lower guard forms
from section 3. Put

```

```

The four-step physical return from the first 3:WL to 3:ER gives

```
tau(3:ER)=w+4,
tau(4:SR)=w+1, tau(first 4:SL)=w+5.
```

The unique east arrival on level five comes from 3:WR -> 2:NR -> 5:E1.
Its lifted time is `v+2-N`. Every north departure on level five feeds
a 4:NR that follows the first 4:SL, so its time exceeds `w+4`.
For every positive N,

```
v+2-N < w+4 < time of every north departure on level five.
```

Thus E1 cannot be EL, and no WR can precede it. The row starts ER and
has EL=0. Its north-departure count is `d=1-a+b`, giving the entire row

```
5: ER,(WL,WR)^d,[WL]^(1-c).
```

Let D1 be the first west departure on level six. It feeds 3:WR, so

```
D1 -> 3:WR -> 2:NR -> 5:ER -> 6:V
                 W,N,E,S
```

is a four-step physical first return. Since 5:ER is the first south
departure, V is the first south arrival on six. If D1=SR, it would be
an earlier south arrival. Consequently D1=NL and V=SR is the second
west departure D2. They are consecutive stable events. As there is no
earlier south arrival and D1 is the first west departure, all ports
before D1 are NR; alternation makes exactly one such event possible.
We have forced

```
6 begins NR,NL,SR.
```

Let U be the second north departure on level seven; it feeds the second
north arrival on six, which is D1=NL. Its exact successors are

```
U -> 6:NL -> 3:WR -> 2:NR -> 5:ER -> 6:SR
  -> first 3:WL -> 4:SR -> 1:WR -> 0:NR -> 3:ER
  -> first 4:SL -> first 7:E arrival E1.
```

The direction word is `NWNESWSWNESE`. Its displacement is zero, and
every intermediate level is below seven. Thus U,E1 are consecutive
stable events. U cannot be EL because E1 is the first east arrival.
Therefore U=WR and E1=EL. Since U is the second north departure and
E1 immediately follows it, E1 is the third north departure on seven.

Now there is the eight-step first return

```
D2=6:SR -> first 3:WL -> 4:SR -> 1:WR -> 0:NR
        -> 3:ER -> first 4:SL -> 7:E1=EL -> third 6:N arrival,
```

with direction word `WSWNESEN`. There is no intermediate level-six
event. Its endpoint must be the next stable event after D2, hence it
is NL. Thus six begins `NR,NL,SR,NL`, with a third west departure.
If a=1, level three has only two west arrivals, so this is impossible.

If a=0, this third west departure feeds the second 3:WL, which feeds
the second 4:SL. Level four is

```
SR,SL,NR,SL,[NR]^b.
```

Its first SL, its NR, and its second SL supply, in order, the first
three east arrivals E1,E2,E3 on seven. But the forced four-step path

```
7:E1=EL -> 6:third west departure NL -> second 3:WL
        -> second 4:SL -> 7:E3
```

has direction word `NWSE`, displacement zero, and no intermediate
level-seven event. It makes E1,E3 consecutive stable visits while
E2 must lie strictly between them. This is a contradiction.

All values of a,b,c and every later wake choice are covered. Only the
clock inequality in the first paragraph uses N, and it uses just N>0.
No growth-eight residue condition, period congruence, width bound, or
lifetime bound is needed. Therefore **a finite-support periodic highway
with drift (1,3) cannot have a lower guard cap at any growth**.

This proof does not transfer to the upper guard: after a half-turn the
clock term `v+2-N` becomes `v+2+N`. The missing proof and the five-row
guard normal form are independent of that sign and do transfer.

#### 5. Two lower (1,2,3) third-level words are impossible

Here levels one and two are respectively `WR,WL` and `NR,SL,NR`.
Write `u=tau(1:WR)`, `w=tau(1:WL)`, so `w>u`. The middle event
on level two has time `w+1`. The unique east arrival on level three,
fed by `1:WR -> 0:NR -> 3:E`, has time `u+2-N`.

when `omega_3=0`, or the long words

```

k=1: WR,WL,ER,WL,WR,[WL]^(1-omega_3);
k=2: WR,WL,WR,WL,ER,[WL]^(1-omega_3).
```

In the short word, the EL is the second north departure on level three.

level two requires `u+3-N>w+1`. Since lifted times are integers,
`w>=u+1` and `u+3-N>=w+2`, so `N<=u+1-w<=0`, a contradiction.

In the `k=2` long word, the two WR events feed the first and last NR
on level two. The time `z` of its second WR therefore satisfies `z>w`.
The ER follows this WR, so `u+2-N>z`. But `u<w<z` implies
`z>=u+2`, again contradicting `N>0`. This covers both wake bits.

#### 1. Complete third- and fourth-row forms

### The four-step phase condition is redundant for strong lifted clocks

Every Langton-ant step turns by ninety degrees, so movement axes
alternate horizontal and vertical. A closed physical walk has equally
many horizontal and vertical moves, with each number even because east
balances west and north balances south. Its length is therefore a
multiple of four. Successive visits to one physical cell share a time
residue modulo four.

In the canonical positive-skew coordinates `t=3x-y`, even-row arrivals
are vertical and odd-row arrivals are horizontal. Choose representatives
`c_t=(0,-t)`. With literal drift `(1,3)` and `N=2 mod4`, one global time
shift gives the lifted representative phases

```text
phi(t)=0 for even t, 3 for odd t.
```

This applies to either clock gauge `tau(target)-tau(source)=1+sigma*N*dx`,
where `sigma=+1` at the upper cap and `-1` at the lower. From an even row,
E/W steps reach an odd row and have increment three modulo four. From an
odd row, N/S steps reach an even row and have increment one modulo four.
Thus every equality edge has increment `phi(target)-phi(source)` modulo
four, regardless of gauge.

Adding these phases does **not** strengthen an integer clock system
whose stable gaps are already at least four. Given any satisfying integer
clock `tau`, define simultaneously at every event

```text
tau'(q)=4*floor((tau(q)-phi(row(q)))/4)+phi(row(q)).
```

On an equality edge the two expressions inside the floors differ by an
integer, because the adjusted clock increment is a multiple of four.
The exact edge equality is preserved. For adjacent events at one row,
their phases agree and their original clock gap is at least four.
Their floored difference is at least one, so the new gap is again at
least four. No assumption that the equality graph is connected is needed.
The period and all period-floor constraints are unchanged.

Consequently the globally phased strong system is feasible if and only
if the existing strong system is feasible. Under weak gaps of one the
rounding can collapse a strict gap, so this redundancy statement does
not apply to weak clocks.
